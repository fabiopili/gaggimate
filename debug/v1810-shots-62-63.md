# v1.8.10 first shots, 62 and 63: under-delivery closed, a wind-up hump opened

Shots 62 and 63 (2026-08-13, Pure Flow, extraction target 2.0 g/s, capless as intended, volumetric cut near 43.5 g). First shots on the Tier 1 coefficients (`0,0,-0.289,6.074` with slip `0,0,-0.036,0.445`, entered through settings on 2026-08-12) and the v1.8.10 display with the 30 percent upward trim clamp. The log proves both are live: `fl` at full duty against an open circuit reads 5.99, the Tier 1 surface, and the trimmed command exceeded 110 percent of target, impossible on the old clamp.

Grading is from the log only; the operator's ear and cup verdict outrank it per the campaign.

## What the shots were

Both pucks were open and fast-eroding: peak pressure only 4.2 bar (62) and 6.1 bar (63), resistance falling from about 2.7 to 0.7 over each extraction. The 9 to 11 bar knee that broke shots 60 and 61 was therefore not revisited; today grades the change at ordinary pressures on eroding pucks.

Baselines: shots 60/61 settled RMS 0.56/0.73 (the failure being fixed); shots 56/57 RMS 0.21/0.23 (the quiet band). Both weights landed within 0.1 g of the 43.5 g target. Bloom held quietly under its 2 bar cap in both.

## What the change fixed

The first-half under-delivery is gone. Yesterday both shots sat at 1.4 to 1.8 g/s for eight seconds and more with the trim pinned; today real flow reached target within 4 to 5 seconds of first drips, the trim actively used +18 and +27 percent without ever touching its new 30 percent bound, duty moved smoothly with no cuts, and nothing in the trace looks unstable.

## The new residual: saturation-window wind-up

The dominant error is now a mid-shot over-delivery hump, 2.4 to 2.7 g/s through the middle third. Its mechanism is visible in shot 63: while the puck was still saturating after first drips (cup flow 0.7 to 1.2, genuinely below pump flow, pressure creeping too slowly for the slew gate to block), the trim read the gap as under-delivery and wound up to +27 percent, and that wind-up discharged as overshoot once the puck opened. The old 10 percent clamp used to bound exactly this at the cost of yesterday's failure; the wider clamp trades it back. Shot 62 unwound and closed its final third at RMS 0.19; shot 63 carried about +0.45 to the cut, helped along by a puck whose resistance kept falling all shot.

Net: settled RMS improved from 0.56/0.73 to 0.35/0.46, weights are exact, and the residual has one clear mechanism rather than several.

## Verdict and next candidate

Qualified pass, pending the ear and cup report. The change did what it was shipped to do and introduced no instability; the miss against the 0.2 to 0.3 band has a single attributable cause that the old clamp was masking rather than solving.

The fix for what these shots showed is a hold-off on upward integration while cup flow is still converging on the command, for example allowing up-integration only once `vf` reaches about 80 percent of the commanded flow, so the saturation gap stops charging the integral. This is the same family as the parked slew-gate hold-off (queue item 2) and should land as one small display OTA, gated on a repeat of today's shots. The knee regime and the v1.8.9 ceiling gate both still await a genuinely tight puck day.

A note for the record: `pf` and `ev` read zero across shots 56, 57, 60, 62 and 63; shot 61 is the only recent shot where the controller's puck-flow estimate engaged. Root cause found in `PressureController::virtualScale()`: the estimator computes puck flow as model pump flow minus a compliance term (a principled observer), but only emits once a hand-tuned state machine sees puck conductance fall faster than 0.5 per second and then settle. Most real shots never produce that sharp transient, so it stays silent; shot 61 triggered it because the shot started against 9.8 bar of residual pressure from shot 60 minutes earlier. The observer's structure is sound and its trigger is brittle. This becomes relevant to the trim architecture question, see the discussion of 2026-08-13.
