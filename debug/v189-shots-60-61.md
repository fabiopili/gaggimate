# v1.8.9 test day one, shots 60 and 61: capless flow tracking fails

Shots 60 and 61 (2026-08-12, Pure Flow, extraction target 2.0 g/s, volumetric cut near 43.5 g, interim coefficients 5.3,3.3 with slip zeroed). The only preparation difference was a slightly coarser grind for 61. Controller and display both on v1.8.9, per the operator.

A correction to the first grading of the day, which called these shots void because the pressure ceiling was not in force. The operator's reading is right: a flow phase with unlimited pressure is a legitimate profile whose flow target the machine should honour, these shots are a valid test of exactly that, and the machine failed it. The v1.8.9 arbitration change itself remains untested either way, since with no ceiling the latch never engages; that specific gate still needs a run with a binding ceiling to compare against shots 58/59, but that is a separate matter from what these shots showed.

## Verdict: fail. The flow phase never held its target

Real flow at the cup swept from about 0.8 up to 3.0 g/s through the 2.0 target and never settled on it. Settled RMS of `vf` against `tf` is 0.56 (shot 60) and 0.73 (shot 61) against the campaign's 0.2 to 0.3 band. Both shots spent most of the extraction at 10 to 11 bar (peaks 11.2 and 11.0, with 16.8 s and 11.5 s above 9), which also explains the cups.

## Mechanism

`fl` carries almost no plant information. As the FlowTrimmer header records, the controller executes flow targets open loop by inverting its pump model and reports back the same model evaluated forward, so `fl` is an echo of the trimmed command and `vf` is the only real flow observation in the system. The `fl`/`vf` divergence is therefore the uncorrected tracking error itself, and it ran in both directions within a single shot.

Early hold: at 11 bar and duty near 78 the interim model over-predicts. The 5.3,3.3 pair is a line through 1 and 9 bar; extrapolated to 11 bar it promises about 2.2 g/s where the pump really delivered 1.4 to 1.8. The trim saw under-delivery and pinned its upward clamp, `MAX_TRIM_UP_RATIO` 0.10, visible in the log as `fl` sitting at exactly 2.20 for seconds at a time. The clamp was sized for a model that under-predicted everywhere then measured; 11 bar is a regime the machine had never reached before, and there the sign flips.

Late hold: the puck eroded under 11 bar (shot 60's resistance estimate spiked to 120.66 just after the bloom, and both shots' `pr` climbed steadily), conductance rose continuously, and real flow climbed through and past the target. The trim reversed and cut the command from 2.2 down to about 1.2 with duty from 78 to below 35, well inside its 75 percent downward authority, but the outer loop is deliberately slow because of the scale's BLE lag, and duty cuts barely moved pressure against this load (78 to 34 duty dropped cp only 10.9 to 9.3), so flow authority was weak exactly where it was needed. The loop chased a plant that was opening faster than it could cut.

So the failure is the pump model detaching from reality in an uncalibrated regime, first one way then the other, with the trim's upward clamp binding in the first half and its bandwidth binding in the second. The controller honoured the flow target as well as its instruments allowed; its primary instrument was wrong by a factor of up to 2.4.

## Decision

A head-to-head computation kills the tempting answer. The two-point path and the poly path are the same model: `setPumpFlowCoeff(5.3, 3.3)` builds the polynomial `{0, 0, -0.25, 5.55}`, against Tier 1's `{0, 0, -0.289, 6.074}` with slip `{0, 0, -0.036, 0.445}`. Evaluated at today's failure points they agree within a few percent: at duty 78 and 11 bar the interim predicts 2.20 g/s and Tier 1 predicts 2.24 where the pump delivered about 1.55, and at duty 35 and 9.2 bar they predict 1.13 and 1.11 where the cup got 2.82. Both are affine in pressure and neither was measured near the pump's knee, so swapping coefficients does not fix what these shots showed, and no two-point pair can without wrecking the calibrated mid-band.

Tier 1 still goes in, as the standing configuration rather than as the fix: it is the measured surface, it refines the calibrated band and low-duty behaviour through the slip term, it retires the interim stopgap, and it is one settings save with the old pair one save away. Since the save-time plausibility guard has not shipped, the transposition risk that produced shot 48 is mitigated manually: read the settings back after saving and export a snapshot before the first shot. Gate: the next normal shot must sit in the shots 56/57 band.

The actual fix candidates for capless flow tracking are code, in ship-small order. First, widen the trim's upward authority (`MAX_TRIM_UP_RATIO`, currently 0.10): the early half of both shots under-delivered specifically because the clamp pinned while the model over-promised at the knee, and the scale is the only instrument that sees the truth up there. One line, one OTA, gated on a capless shot like today's. Second, optionally, a short rig sweep at 9 to 11 bar into the nearly closed steam wand to measure the knee properly (measurement only, no loop retuning per the standing rule), which would let the cubic terms actually be nonzero where the surface bends. The late-shot over-delivery on an eroding puck is bandwidth and plant physics; no static model fixes it, and only a ceiling bounds it.

The v1.8.9 ceiling-hold gate stays open and unrelated: grading it against the shots 58/59 baseline requires a binding ceiling, so that comparison run needs an explicit 9 bar on the extraction phase for the test only. Whether the everyday Pure Flow profile carries a ceiling as protection against 11 bar days is the operator's call, not a firmware requirement.

## Watch items

If it recurs it deserves a code look. Capless at duty 79 the machine reached 11.2 bar, the closest observation yet of the open ceiling; the 11.5 bar OPV was approached but not clearly hit. The two v1.8.8 display gates (editor warning on a 0 ceiling, finished-screen weight hold) are now installable and still ungraded.
